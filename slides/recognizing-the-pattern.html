01 / 10

The core

Grandiosity, a constant need for admiration,
and absent empathy — pervasive, and lifelong.

Grandiose · overt

“This place would fall apart without me.”

Loud, dominating, visibly self-important. Easy to spot, and so it gets all the attention.

Covert · vulnerable

“No one has any idea what I’ve had to carry.”

Quiet, wounded, the perpetual victim. Harder to name, because it arrives asking for your sympathy.

Same engine

Only the volume differs. The entitlement, and the missing empathy, are identical.

Everything that follows is how this shows up on an ordinary day

Where it shows up

It is almost never one dramatic thing.
It is the same small move, on an ordinary day.

In conversation

“You ran 5K? I did a half-marathon on a sprained ankle.”

One-upping · your news becomes their stage

You mention a hard week. Two sentences later they’re describing a harder one. Yours never comes back.

Conversational redirect · no genuine curiosity about you

A question about their weekend returns a list of important people who were there.

Name-dropping · the answer is always the audience

In relationships

Week one: “I have never felt this before.” Week six: cold, unexplained — and you’re the one chasing.

Love bombing, then cooling · the chase is the point

You say “I felt hurt when…” and somehow end the conversation apologising.

Your needs re-read as an attack on them

They read your feelings perfectly — and still do the thing.

Cognitive empathy, not affective · they know. It was never a misunderstanding.

Pattern over incident — any one of these, once, is just a bad day

Under criticism

The most revealing thirty seconds
you will ever get.

Say one mild, true thing — “hey, that stung” — and watch what comes back. Someone with a self worth having gets briefly uncomfortable, then curious. Here is the other reply, in its four usual costumes.

“I’m sorry you feel that way.”

Non-apology · grammatically an apology, functionally a verdict on you

“I’m sorry, but you—”

The apology that becomes an indictment before the sentence ends

“After everything I’ve done for you, this is what you bring up?”

DARVO · deny, attack, and arrive as the injured party

Three days of silence. Then normality, as if nothing was raised.

Narcissistic injury · rage or ice. Reflection is the one thing that never comes.

You asked for very little — which is exactly what makes the size of the reaction data

At work

The waiter
test.

How someone treats a person who has no power over their life is the most honest information you will ever get about them.

Charming upward · dismissive downward

The team ships. “I drove this.”
The project slips. “My junior dropped the ball.”

Credit travels up · blame travels down

“That policy isn’t really meant for someone at my level.”

Rules are for other people · the exception always applies to them

They take apart a colleague who isn’t in the room, and you laugh along.

This is the sound of you, after you leave the room

Over years: a trail of “crazy” exes, “toxic” bosses, “backstabbing” friends — and never one word of their own part in it

05 / 10

The vocabulary

Thirteen moves. Name it and it loses power.

The cycle — in order

01Love bombing
Overwhelming affection, gifts, future-planning at the start. Builds dependency and lowers your guard.
02Devaluation
Warmth withdraws; criticism, comparison and coldness replace it. You start performing to get phase one back.
03Discard
Abrupt exit or emotional shutdown once you stop being useful — often once new supply is secured.
04Hoovering
Sucking you back after the discard: crisis calls, apologies, nostalgia, “I’ve changed.”

↻ and then straight back to 01

Attacks on reality

Gaslighting
Denying facts and distorting events until you doubt your own memory and perception.
DARVO
Deny, Attack, Reverse Victim and Offender. You confront them; they deny; they attack you for raising it; they finish as the wounded party.
Projection
Accusing you of precisely what they are doing — lying, disloyalty, selfishness.
Word salad
Circular, topic-hopping conflict that is exhausting by design. Nothing resolves; you give up. That was the win.

Pressure tools

Silent treatment
Punishment by withdrawal, forcing you to cave to restore contact.
Future faking
The marriage, the promotion, the trip, the change — promised to buy compliance now, never delivered.
Moving the goalposts
The standard shifts the moment you meet it, so you can never arrive.
Triangulation
A third person imported — “even they agree with me” — to destabilise you and create competition.
Smear campaign
Rewriting your reputation to your allies in advance, so your account is discredited before you give it.

DARVO is Jennifer Freyd’s term — the rest are the ordinary working vocabulary of the field

The fastest test

They can manage their presentation.
They cannot manage your nervous system.

This list is more reliable than any list of their traits, because it is made of the one thing they don’t have access to.

  • You feel anxious before seeing them, and drained afterwards.
  • You rehearse sentences before saying them, and edit yourself constantly.
  • You increasingly doubt your own memory of events.
  • You over-explain and over-apologise — including for things that were never yours.
  • You leave conversations confused, and can’t reconstruct why.
  • Your world has quietly shrunk: fewer friends, less family, hobbies dropped.
  • You’ve started keeping screenshots and notes, just to stay sane.
  • The good moments feel like relief rather than joy.
  • People who know you say you seem different lately.
Three or more — act on it. Whatever the label turns out to be, and whether or not anyone is ever diagnosed. The label was never the thing you needed. Getting your footing back is.

Early behavioural flags: intensity out of proportion to time known · every ex is “crazy” · contempt for anyone below them · your boundary read as betrayal

Guard rails

The word is badly over-used.
Here is where it doesn’t apply.

Confidence, ambition, high standards Wanting a lot and asking for it plainly is not the pattern.

The difference · healthy self-esteem is stable. Grandiosity is brittle and needs an audience.

Someone having the worst year of their life Grief, burnout, depression, addiction and acute stress all produce real self-absorption.

The difference · a bad season, not a personality structure

Neurodivergence Autistic and ADHD traits — missed cues, blunt delivery, intense interests — get mislabelled constantly.

The difference · intent and exploitation. Not perceiving your feelings is not the same as perceiving them and using them.

One person’s account of a conflict — including yours Abusers accuse other people of being narcissists. That move has a name on the previous page.

The difference · DARVO looks exactly like this from the inside

Someone you are simply very angry at The label is a tool, and a tool can be picked up for the wrong job.

The difference · if it’s doing emotional work rather than descriptive work, it’s the wrong tool

Diagnosis belongs to a qualified clinician — recognition belongs to you

08 / 10

If someone in your life fits this

You can’t fix it from the outside.
You can change your exposure to it.

Stop trying to win it.

Insight is the missing part — you can’t supply it from outside. Chasing the admission is what keeps people hooked for years.

Act, don’t announce.

“I won’t discuss this while you’re shouting” — and then actually leaving the room. Worth more than any speech about respect.

Starve the reaction.

For contact you can’t avoid — grey rock: calm tone, short factual answers, no emotion, no detail. Caveat: under-studied, and it can escalate cyclically abusive situations.

Write it down.

Dated notes, saved messages. This is for your own mind first — it’s also what matters if it ever becomes an HR or legal question.

Rebuild what got eroded.

Isolation is the mechanism, not a side effect. Reconnect with the people who knew you before.

Plan the hoover now.

It arrives after any distance. Decide your answer today, while you’re calm — not at one in the morning, when you’re lonely.

Get your own support.

A therapist who knows this pattern. Avoid couples counselling as a first step if there is active abuse — joint sessions get weaponised.

Keep two people fully informed.

Someone who gets the unedited version. A smear campaign only works on an audience that hasn’t heard from you.

If there are threats or physical intimidation, this stops being a conversation about personality traits and becomes one about safety. Contact a domestic-abuse helpline — in India, the women’s helpline is 181 and emergency services are 112.

Boundaries are things you do, not things you declare

Worth remembering
  • 01Grandiosity, a need for admiration, no empathy — pervasive and lifelong. That is the core.
  • 02Two faces: loud and grandiose, quiet and wounded. Same engine.
  • 03Watch how they answer mild criticism. Thirty seconds, and you know.
  • 04Watch how they treat people who cannot help them.
  • 05Behaviour over words. Pattern over incident.
  • 06A trail of discarded people and no self-accountability anywhere is the signal.
  • 07Track how you feel around them — that data is harder to manipulate than anything they say.
